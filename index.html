The Risk: When It Is Circular If a system sets level difficulty strictly as a function of your observed win rate: Difficulty 𝑖 = 1 − WinRate 𝑖 Difficulty i ​ =1−WinRate i ​ And then calculates your player rating from that difficulty: Rating = ∑ Difficulty 𝑖 × Attempts 𝑖 Rating=∑Difficulty i ​ ×Attempts i ​ This creates strict circular reasoning: If you spend two weeks practicing Level 10 and master it, your win rate on Level 10 rises from 20 % 20% to 70 % 70%. A naive model would conclude: "Level 10 has a 70% win rate, therefore Level 10 is an easy drill (difficulty drops from 10.2 to 4.0)." As a result, your calculated skill rating decreases because you got better. That is the classic circular trap. The Solution: Item Response Theory (IRT) & Bayesian Updating Difficulty estimation from user data is not circular if structured using an Anchored Prior Model (analogous to the Rasch Model in psychometrics or Glicko in chess): Objective Physics Prior (Anchor Scale): The baseline difficulty vector ( 1.0 , 2.0 , … , 16.0 1.0,2.0,…,16.0) is grounded in objective table physics (ball count, pocket geometry, presence of rotation, number of BIH bailouts). This fixes the absolute scale and prevents the whole system from floating.
